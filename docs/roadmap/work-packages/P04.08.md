# P04.08 — Background-Job Ownership, Tenant Context & Correlation Contract

- Phase: `P04`
- State: `prepared / planned / locked`
- Owner/domain: `kernel.jobs`
- Depends on: accepted `P04.01` envelope/correlation/causation contract, accepted `P04.02` route/ownership contract, accepted `P04.07` schema boundary, accepted P01.09 job and scheduler primitives, retained P02/P03 tenant-context and authorization authority, and a later separate governed terminal-checkpoint/P04.08 activation transaction
- Blocks: `P04.09`
- Change class: `B`

> This document prepares the P04.08 contract only. It is **not implementation authority**. P04 remains at the ADR-0013 implementation-locked intra-phase terminal checkpoint with `current_work_package = null`, `kernel_code_authorized = false` and `business_feature_code_authorized = false`. No P04.08 runtime, durable job store, worker loop, job migration, business handler, correlation runtime, provider selection or canonical state mutation may begin until a later separate governance/state/continuity transaction explicitly accepts this contract, makes P04.08 the sole active package, passes exact-head Omnexa Governance, merges through the protected path, and protected `main` is re-read.

## Objective

Define the minimum provider-neutral contract for **owning background jobs**, binding them to an explicit, validated tenant context, and carrying event correlation/causation identity across job execution — so that background work is attributable, tenant-isolated, duplicate-safe and diagnosable without inheriting authorization from its transport.

P04.08 must extend accepted foundations rather than replace them:

- P01.09 remains authoritative for the in-process job type registry, bounded executor, execution identity, retry-policy shape and schedule vocabulary. P01.09 is deliberately process-local and makes no durability claim; P04.08 must not silently convert it into a durable runtime by wording alone;
- P04.01 remains authoritative for the canonical event envelope, EventID, event type identity, envelope version, and the `correlationid` / `causationid` semantics;
- P04.02 remains authoritative for producer/route/consumer registration and ownership;
- P02 remains authoritative for tenant identity, trusted tenant context and session/actor establishment;
- P02/P03 remain authoritative for authorization, capability and permission enforcement;
- P04.03 checkpoint, P04.04 outbox, P04.05 inbox/idempotency, P04.06 retry/quarantine and P04.07 schema registry/validation remain separate facts;
- job execution evidence proves only that a registered job type ran under a bounded attempt contract with an explicit owner/tenant/correlation identity. It does **not** authorize an action, prove exactly-once processing, prove business success, establish global ordering or grant cross-module write authority.

This package closes background-job ownership, tenant-context binding and correlation propagation only. It does not change reliability observability or operator recovery UX (`P04.09`), aggregate replay/duplicate/restart/poison-event acceptance (`P04.10`), or any business feature/module.

No job ID, job type, execution ID, owner label, tenant identifier, correlation ID, causation ID, claim receipt, idempotency key, scheduler receipt or queue admission grants authorization.

## Job ownership law

Every background job execution must be attributable to exactly one authoritative owner before it is eligible to run.

Minimum requirements:

1. job type registration remains owner-governed: only the authoritative module/owner may register a handler for its job types through the accepted registry/change boundary, and registration is frozen before worker execution begins under retained P01.09 rules;
2. each registered job type carries one stable authoritative owner/module identity; a caller-provided owner string, payload label, tenant hint or provider tag can never select, override or rebind that owner;
3. a job request that does not resolve to a registered job type fails closed before execution;
4. owner identity must be resolvable from accepted repository-local registration state, not from job payload content;
5. cross-owner execution of an owner-private job type fails closed, including when the caller supplies a valid tenant context;
6. job execution may not be enqueued, claimed or executed outside the registered handler boundary — no dynamic handler loading, reflection-based dispatch of unregistered types, plugin resolution, shell invocation or code evaluation derived from job data;
7. scheduler/executor identity is infrastructure identity only: it never implies module ownership, capability, role or tenant membership;
8. ownership does not transfer to a downstream module merely because that module observed, retried or logged the job.

Owner scope is mandatory and cannot be replaced by a caller-provided label. A later public/admin submission API, if any, requires separate capability/authorization/audit design; this preparation does not authorize one.

## Tenant context law

Background jobs must reconstruct and carry an explicit tenant context instead of assuming a global tenant or trusting a payload-supplied tenant value.

Requirements:

1. tenant context for a job is explicit, structured, validated and fail-closed — a missing, malformed, inactive, cross-tenant or unsupported tenant reference blocks execution before protected mutation;
2. tenant context must be derived from the accepted trusted tenant/actor establishment path, never from unvalidated job payload fields, provider metadata or caller-controlled strings;
3. a tenant context bound at enqueue time must still be revalidated at claim/execution time under accepted P02 rules; stale or revoked tenant context fails closed;
4. tenant context is separate from authorization: possessing a valid tenant context never implies a capability, role or permission, and every protected action still evaluates authorization at its own boundary;
5. one tenant's jobs, payloads, schedules, claims, results, diagnostics and history are not readable, mutable, cancellable or enumerable by another tenant;
6. a job with no legitimate tenant scope must be explicitly classified as platform/system-scoped under accepted platform rules rather than defaulting to an implicit "all tenants" context;
7. tenant context must not be used as an idempotency scope substitute: an idempotency key is scoped by exact owner/type/tenant binding, and identical keys in different tenants must not collide, deduplicate across tenants or reveal one another's existence;
8. tenant context travels with correlation identity but does not change it; correlation propagation must never widen tenant visibility.

Fail-closed behavior is mandatory: unknown/unresolvable tenant context is a stable non-success outcome, not an implicit platform-mode fallback.

## Correlation and causation law

P04.08 extends accepted P04.01 correlation/causation semantics to background jobs rather than inventing a second tracing identity.

Requirements:

1. `correlationid` groups one wider logical business interaction; `causationid` identifies the immediate triggering message/event/request — the two must remain distinct in job context exactly as they are in the event envelope;
2. a job triggered by an event inherits the event's correlation identity and records the triggering event/command identity as its causation identity;
3. a job started at a new logical root interaction establishes a new correlation identity explicitly; correlation is never silently synthesized per attempt, per worker or per retry;
4. an existing correlation identity is preserved across the logical chain and is not overwritten merely because a different module, owner, tenant hop or worker executed the attempt;
5. correlation identity is immutable for a given job execution; retries and re-deliveries of the same execution do not mint a new correlation identity, and a deliberately new execution requires an explicit new identity decision;
6. correlation/causation identifiers are opaque identity values: they are never authorization credentials, never used as idempotency keys, and never used as tenant selectors;
7. correlation/causation values must be validated as the accepted identifier form and bounded in length; malformed or unsupported values fail closed rather than being partially accepted or coerced;
8. correlation identity must not be treated as a global ordering device: a shared correlation ID proves no ordering, no completeness of the chain and no exactly-once relationship between members;
9. where propagation crosses a process boundary, values are carried in the accepted envelope/context form — not reconstructed from logs, provider headers or payload guesses.

## Job identity, idempotency and duplicate-safety law

Background execution remains duplicate-safe by construction rather than by provider promise.

Requirements:

1. execution identity remains the accepted stable job execution identity (UUIDv7 under retained P01.09 rules); it is opaque, non-semantic and non-authorizing;
2. one logical job submission under an explicit idempotency contract converges to one logical outcome within its exact owner/type/tenant scope;
3. idempotency key and fingerprint remain opaque caller-supplied contract values under retained P01.09 rules — they are not entity IDs, tenant IDs, correlation IDs or authorization tokens;
4. duplicate admission, duplicate claim or duplicate attempt delivery is explicitly possible and must be safe: protected side effects remain idempotent under retained P04.05 rules;
5. job idempotency, P04.05 inbox completion and P04.06 retry disposition remain distinct facts with distinct storage and evidence semantics; none of them substitutes for another;
6. a duplicate job attempt must not fabricate a success, a checkpoint advance, an inbox completion or an audit success for a different identity;
7. scheduler restart, process restart or queue re-admission must not convert an already-resolved job execution into a fresh unrelated execution;
8. no claim, receipt, future handle or completion callback may be reused across owner, type, tenant or correlation boundaries.

## Claim, lease and concurrency law

If a later authorized implementation introduces durable or multi-worker claiming, it must remain bounded and fail-closed.

Requirements:

1. claiming a job execution is an atomic compare-and-set/lease operation: at most one active claimer per execution under the accepted lease rules;
2. an interrupted or expired claim does not create a second concurrent owner; reclaim must follow explicit lease-expiry rules and must not falsely consume attempt budget or mark progress that did not occur;
3. lease duration, renewal and expiry are explicit, bounded, deterministic values; unbounded or provider-default leases are forbidden;
4. claim state is execution-scoped and must never be inferred from correlation identity, tenant hints or payload content;
5. concurrent submission of the same idempotent job may converge safely only for the same exact identity/scope; conflicting fingerprint/identity combinations fail closed;
6. at-least-once execution remains the honest baseline: no queue, worker count, lease or scheduler configuration may be described as exactly-once;
7. worker concurrency limits are bounded configuration, not capacity guarantees, and exceeding them delays work rather than dropping it silently;
8. claim/lease evidence is diagnostic state only and grants no authorization, no tenant visibility and no business-success meaning.

## Scheduling, cancellation and lifecycle law

P04.08 must keep scheduling semantics small, explicit and bounded.

Requirements:

1. the schedule vocabulary stays explicit (one-shot, bounded recurring interval) under retained P01.09 normalization rules — UTC instants, bounded intervals, no implicit calendar/timezone reinterpretation in the kernel contract;
2. scheduling produces eligibility to run, not proof that work ran, succeeded or was authorized;
3. cancellation and shutdown are explicit states; a canceled/stopped executor must not report success for work that did not complete, and in-flight attempts must follow the accepted interruption semantics without inventing attempt consumption;
4. deadline/timeout behavior is bounded and produces a stable safe terminal reason rather than raw handler/provider text;
5. a schedule, timer or wake-up is never a business approval, a workflow wait, or a substitute for the later P05 workflow timer semantics;
6. no cron/expression language, dynamic code or provider-specific scheduling DSL is introduced by this package; if a richer trigger surface is ever required, it needs separate governed design.

## Failure, retry and quarantine composition law

Job failure handling must compose with P04.06 rather than duplicate it.

Requirements:

1. P01.09 retains the bounded deterministic in-process retry-policy shape; P04.06 remains authoritative for durable failure disposition, attempt budget, eligibility, terminal failure and quarantine/poison-event state;
2. P04.08 must not invent a parallel retry ledger, a second attempt counter or a competing eligibility clock alongside P04.06;
3. where durable job attempt state is later authorized, the implementation must state explicitly which facts are P04.06-owned and which are job-execution-owned, and prove they cannot diverge into contradictory truth;
4. terminal job failure is a stable safe outcome with a bounded machine reason; raw handler errors, stack traces, provider text and payload content are not retained as public evidence;
5. quarantine/poison disposition is never bypassed by re-enqueueing the same execution under a new identity to escape an attempt budget;
6. job failure never creates authorization, inbox completion, checkpoint success or schema-validation success;
7. validation failure from P04.07 and job failure from P04.08 remain separately classifiable stable outcomes.

## Isolation and multi-tenant safety law

A later implementation must prove:

- one tenant cannot enqueue, claim, cancel, inspect, enumerate or replay another tenant's jobs;
- an owner cannot execute another owner's registered job type without an accepted capability boundary;
- cross-tenant same-type job names cannot collide into shared state where accepted canonical identity differs;
- job payloads, results, schedules and diagnostics remain classified, bounded and tenant-scoped;
- queue partitioning, sharding or worker assignment may never be derived from caller-supplied tenant hints;
- diagnostic reads remain capability/tenant/owner bounded;
- no hidden super-admin or operator bypass is introduced to observe "all jobs" outside accepted platform-scoped rules and audit.

If a future product requirement needs cross-tenant operational visibility, it requires separate governed platform-operations design with explicit audit and cannot be smuggled into P04.08 V1.

## Separation of facts

Six facts remain distinct:

1. P04.03 checkpoint = ordered durable consumption progress.
2. P04.04 outbox = transactional publication intent.
3. P04.05 inbox completion = duplicate-safe protected local mutation evidence.
4. P04.06 retry/quarantine = failure disposition, attempt budget, eligibility and terminal poison-event evidence.
5. P04.07 schema registry/validation = payload contract identity, compatibility and validation evidence.
6. P04.08 job execution = owner-attributed, tenant-scoped, correlation-bound background work progress.

Consequences:

- a job starting, claiming, retrying or completing does not advance a checkpoint;
- job completion does not create inbox completion, outbox publication or schema registration;
- correlation continuity does not prove that any member of the chain succeeded, was authorized or even ran;
- a scheduled job firing does not prove a business action occurred;
- job idempotency does not replace P04.05 inbox dedupe or P04.06 attempt budgets;
- none of these facts grants authorization or proves exactly-once/global ordering.

P04.08 also does not implement P04.09 reliability observability/diagnostics/operator recovery UX or P04.10 aggregate replay/duplicate/failure/restart/poison-event acceptance controls.

## Security, privacy and observability law

- job diagnostics must follow accepted data classification and P01.03/P01.07 safe-error/logging rules;
- payloads, secrets, tokens, credentials, raw database/provider errors and unrestricted stack traces are prohibited from persisted/public job evidence, terminal reasons and metrics;
- safe telemetry may include bounded owner/module identity, job type, execution state/reason, attempt count, tenant identifier only where policy allows, correlation identifier only where policy allows, and duration buckets;
- high-cardinality raw execution IDs, tenant IDs, user IDs or correlation IDs must not be blindly used as metric labels;
- audit evidence is required for privileged job administration/cancellation/inspection in any later authorized implementation;
- job execution must not deserialize into executable types in a way that triggers arbitrary constructors/hooks/code;
- queue/job data is never a source of authorization rules, capabilities, roles or tenant membership;
- credential-safe verification environments remain mandatory: unrelated host/provider credentials must never be inherited by job test fixtures or verifiers.

## Required tests / acceptance criteria

Before later P04.08 promotion, the authorized implementation must prove on its exact final head at least:

1. a job type resolves exactly one authoritative owner and cannot be rebound by caller/payload metadata;
2. an unregistered job type fails closed before execution, including under adversarial type input;
3. cross-owner execution of an owner-private job type fails closed;
4. no dynamic handler loading, plugin resolution, reflection dispatch of unregistered types, shell or code execution is reachable from job data;
5. missing/malformed/inactive tenant context fails closed before protected mutation;
6. a payload-supplied tenant value cannot override the accepted trusted tenant context;
7. tenant context is revalidated at claim/execution time and stale/revoked context fails closed;
8. valid tenant context grants no capability, role or permission by itself;
9. cross-tenant job submission, claim, cancel, inspection and enumeration fail closed;
10. idempotent job keys do not collide or dedupe across tenants and do not reveal cross-tenant existence;
11. event-triggered jobs inherit correlation identity and record the immediate triggering identity as causation;
12. new logical roots establish an explicit new correlation identity without overwriting an existing chain;
13. retries/re-deliveries of one execution do not mint a new correlation identity;
14. malformed/oversized correlation or causation values fail closed rather than being coerced;
15. correlation identity is never accepted as an authorization, idempotency or tenant selector input;
16. duplicate admission/claim/attempt delivery is possible and remains safe for protected side effects;
17. job idempotency, P04.05 inbox completion and P04.06 attempt/quarantine state cannot be confused for one another;
18. concurrent claims of one execution yield exactly one active claim under the accepted lease rules;
19. interrupted/expired claims reclaim correctly without false attempt consumption or false progress;
20. restart preserves accepted execution identity, tenant binding, correlation identity and attempt budget (if durable state is authorized);
21. no exactly-once claim appears in code, comments, docs or tests for queue/job execution;
22. terminal job failures return stable bounded reasons without leaking payload/provider/database text;
23. cancellation/stop/deadline paths never report success for incomplete work;
24. metric/log labels respect cardinality and classification limits;
25. no queue/broker/scheduler/provider selection, migration 4 assumption, P04.09/P04.10 runtime, business feature or AI/model/agent product runtime appears in the diff;
